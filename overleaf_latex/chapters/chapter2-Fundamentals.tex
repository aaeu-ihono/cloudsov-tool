\section{The EU Digital Decade and the 2030 Digital Compass}
\label{sec:digital_decade}
% TODO: expand this section
% Source: \url{https://eufordigital.eu/discover-eu/eu-digital-strategy/}

The EU Digital Decade Policy Programme~\cite{EC_DigitalDecade_2022}, established by Decision~(EU)~2022/2481 of the European Parliament and of the Council, sets the Union's overarching digital transformation agenda through~2030. Structured around four cardinal points --- digital skills, digital infrastructure, digitalisation of businesses, and digitalisation of public services --- the programme translates the Commission's 2021 Digital Compass communication into legally binding trajectory targets and an annual monitoring mechanism. For cloud computing specifically, the programme targets 75\% of EU enterprises using cloud services, artificial intelligence, or big data by 2030, and at least 80\% of the EU population holding basic digital skills~\cite{EC_DigitalDecade_2022}. A parallel target requires EU Member States to migrate all digitisable public services online and to offer them interoperably across borders by the same year.

The relevance of the Digital Decade to cloud sovereignty is direct. If 75\% of EU enterprises and the majority of Member State administrations are to operate on cloud infrastructure by 2030, the origin, jurisdiction, and governance of that infrastructure become policy questions of the first order. An EU enterprise that meets the Digital Decade cloud target by migrating workloads to a US-headquartered hyperscaler may satisfy the letter of the programme's digitalisation objective while simultaneously creating the CLOUD~Act exposure and data-transfer vulnerabilities discussed in Section~\ref{sec:eu_regulatory_landscape}. This tension --- between \emph{digital adoption} as measured by the Digital Decade scoreboard and \emph{digital sovereignty} as assessed by frameworks such as the one developed in this thesis --- is the principal motivation for examining EU-origin cloud providers not merely as a market segment, but as a strategic infrastructure class.
