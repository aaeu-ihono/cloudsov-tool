## 9. EU Cloud Market Context & 2030 Projections

> Sources: [Synergy Research Group](https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/european-cloud-providers-local-market-share-now-holds-steady-at-15); [IDC](https://www.idc.com); [MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/europe-cloud-computing-market-42100414.html); [Fierce Network — 2025 forecast](https://www.fierce-network.com/cloud/europes-cloud-market-poised-24-growth)

### EU Provider Share — Structural Decline and Stabilisation

| Year | Total EU Cloud Market | EU Provider Share | EU Provider Revenue (est.) | Hyperscaler Share |
|------|-----------------------|------------------|--------------------------|------------------|
| 2017 | ~€10B | **~29%** | ~€2.9B | ~55% |
| 2022 | ~€35B | **~15%** | ~€5.3B | ~68% |
| 2024 | **€61B** | **~15%** | **~€9.2B** | **~70%** |
| 2025 (forecast) | **~€76B** (+24%) | ~15% (est.) | ~€11.4B | ~70% (est.) |

> **Critical finding:** EU providers' revenue more than tripled since 2017, but their market *share* collapsed from 29% (2017) to 15% (2022) and has been **stable at ~15% since**. Hyperscalers expanded the total market faster than EU providers could grow. AWS alone (~$128.7B in 2025) exceeds all EU providers combined by an estimated 20–30×.
>
> John Dinsdale (Synergy Research): *"US competitors' massive quarterly capex investments (~€10B/quarter) create an insurmountable competitive barrier. European leaders have mostly settled into positions of serving local groups of customers rather than challenging hyperscaler dominance."*

### 2030 Trajectory Scenarios

| Scenario | Assumption | EU Market 2030 | EU Provider Revenue | AWS 2030 (proj.) | Ratio |
|----------|-----------|---------------|--------------------|--------------------|-------|
| **Base** | 15% EU share maintained; ~11% market CAGR | ~€200B | **~€30B** | ~€250B+ | ~8× |
| **Optimistic** | EU share recovers to 20% (EU AI Act mandate + AION/StackIT delivery) | ~€200B | **~€40B** | ~€250B+ | ~6× |
| **Pessimistic** | EU share falls to 12% (hyperscaler sovereign products absorb regulated workloads) | ~€200B | **~€24B** | ~€250B+ | ~10× |

> **Thesis note:** The 2030 ratio gap narrows (from ~14× in 2024 to ~8× base case), but the *absolute* gap widens — EU providers collectively reach ~€30B while AWS alone approaches ~€250B. The relevant thesis question is whether the EU sovereign cloud *segment* (where AWS cannot participate under CLOUD Act) is large enough to sustain EU providers independently of the total market race. The EU AI Act high-risk provisions (mandatory EU-resident processing, August 2026 application date) are the key structural variable for the optimistic scenario — if they drive enterprise AI workloads to EU-sovereign providers, share recovery becomes plausible.

## 10. Summary Assessment for Thesis

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Revenue data availability** | Excellent — AWS revenue and operating income disclosed separately in annual SEC filings since 2014. Most transparent cloud provider in this study. |
| **Scale** | AWS 2025 revenue (~$128.7B) exceeds the combined annual revenues of all 18 EU providers in this study by a factor estimated at 20–30×. |
| **Growth rate (5-yr CAGR 2020–2025)** | ~23% CAGR — sustained hyperscale growth driven by enterprise migrations and AI workload adoption. |
| **Investment capacity** | Group capex of $83B (2024), rising to $100B+ (2025). AWS can deploy EU annual cloud revenues as a rounding error in infrastructure investment. |
| **Operating margin** | 37% operating margin (2024) — the highest in the global cloud industry. EU providers average 15–30% adjusted EBITDA margins on a smaller base. |
| **EU sovereignty limitation** | CLOUD Act exposure is the structural constraint. AWS's 2024 European Sovereign Cloud announcement addresses *operational* sovereignty (EU-resident operators, EU-only data paths) but does not resolve *jurisdictional* sovereignty (ultimate legal control remains under US law). This is the core reason EU organisations with high regulatory requirements cannot use AWS as their primary sovereign cloud. |
| **Thesis role** | AWS is the benchmark against which the EU gap is measured. The financial analysis should not frame AWS as a competitor to EU sovereign providers — they serve different regulatory environments. The thesis question is: is the EU sovereign cloud market (where AWS cannot participate due to jurisdictional constraints) large enough to sustain the EU providers? |
