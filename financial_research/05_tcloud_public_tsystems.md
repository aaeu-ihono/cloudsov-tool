## 6. Sovereignty Concern — Huawei Dependency

> **This is a material finding for the thesis. A cloud platform marketed as a sovereign European alternative runs on Chinese-supplied technology.**

| Issue | Detail | Source |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Huawei role | Huawei provides: (1) Huawei FusionSphere (cloud OS, an OpenStack distribution); (2) hardware infrastructure for Open Telekom Cloud/T-Cloud Public | [Lightreading — "Huawei meets US tech in Deutsche Telekom's 'sovereign' cloud"](https://www.lightreading.com/cloud/huawei-meets-us-tech-in-deutsche-telekom-s-sovereign-cloud) |
| Sovereignty question | Does Huawei possess a "kill switch" for T-Cloud Public? Is US regulatory dependency simply replaced by Chinese supplier dependency? | [Lightreading coverage](https://www.lightreading.com/cloud/huawei-meets-us-tech-in-deutsche-telekom-s-sovereign-cloud) |
| Deutsche Telekom's justification | "The European alternatives available at that time were often not yet mature or market-ready, while Huawei components led the way in terms of price-performance and speed of innovation." (Statement on the 2015 partnership decision) | [Lightreading](https://www.lightreading.com/cloud/huawei-meets-us-tech-in-deutsche-telekom-s-sovereign-cloud) |
| New direction | Deutsche Telekom is building new cloud products (Industrial AI Cloud) without Huawei or US Big Tech — indicating a strategic shift away from the Huawei dependency for future platforms | [Lightreading — "Deutsche Telekom breaks with Huawei and US Big Tech in new clouds"](https://www.lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/deutsche-telekom-breaks-with-huawei-and-us-big-tech-in-new-clouds) |
| Current status (2026) | T-Cloud Public still runs on Huawei OpenStack stack. No announced date for migration away from Huawei for the existing platform. | — |

> **Thesis implication:** T-Cloud Public's Huawei dependency is a complication for its EC DG DIGIT SOV scoring — particularly under SOV-1 (Data Control) and SOV-5 (Technology Independence). The platform is EU-resident and Germany-operated, but its technology stack introduces a non-EU supply chain dependency that mirrors (in kind, not in legal form) the CLOUD Act concern with US hyperscalers.

> **Forward outlook:** T-Systems neither won the EC sovereign cloud tender nor participates in IPCEI-CIS — placing it outside the two most significant EU public cloud funding programmes of 2023–2026. Its investment trajectory depends entirely on Deutsche Telekom capital allocation decisions, with €229M/yr cloud capex providing steady but unspectacular growth capacity.
>
> The **Industrial AI Cloud** (announced 2026) is the pivotal signal. If Deutsche Telekom builds the Huawei-free platform to production scale and migrates institutional workloads onto it, T-Cloud Public's sovereignty credentials would be substantially repaired. Until a firm timeline and investment commitment are announced for that migration, T-Cloud Public's Huawei FusionSphere dependency (Section 6) remains a structural barrier to winning the highest-assurance EU regulated contracts — the same market that OVHcloud (SecNumCloud) and StackIT (EC tender winner) are currently positioned to capture.

## 12. Summary Assessment for Thesis

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Revenue data availability** | Limited — T-Cloud Public revenue is not disclosed. T-Systems segment revenue (€4.0B in 2024) is available from Deutsche Telekom annual report. Cloud as ~55% of T-Systems revenue (~€2.2B) is a CMD management estimate, not an audited segment figure. |
| **Scale** | T-Systems total revenue (~€4.0B) makes it the largest EU provider in this study by segment revenue — but the majority is IT managed services and digital consulting, not cloud infrastructure. Cloud revenue (~€2.2B including hyperscaler reselling) dwarfs OVHcloud and IONOS, but T-Cloud Public's own sovereign IaaS is a subset of this. |
| **Growth** | T-Systems CAGR 2020–2024 is only ~2% — recovery from a restructuring period. Cloud area growing faster internally (+4% CAGR 2022–2024e) but overall business is IT services at modest growth. |
| **Margin** | EBITDA AL margin ~9.6% — much lower than cloud-pure providers (OVHcloud 40%, AWS 37%). This reflects the IT services and consulting nature of most T-Systems revenue, not infrastructure cloud economics. |
| **Investment** | Low capex (€229M in 2024, ~5.7% of revenue) — T-Systems relies on Deutsche Telekom's group infrastructure and does not build hyperscale data centres independently. Financial backing from €111.6B parent provides stability. |
| **EU sovereignty position** | T-Cloud Public is marketed explicitly as a European sovereign cloud and is the only EU provider in this study that won a major EU programme (Copernicus). However, its Huawei FusionSphere dependency is a structural sovereignty complication — the platform's technology stack is controlled by a Chinese company, raising the same category of third-country jurisdiction concerns that disqualify US hyperscalers from some EU regulated workloads. Deutsche Telekom is building Huawei-free products for future cloud but has not announced migration of T-Cloud Public. |
| **Thesis implication** | T-Systems/T-Cloud Public is the most complex provider in this study. It is simultaneously the largest EU IT services provider and a company with a genuine sovereignty question embedded in its flagship sovereign cloud platform. The Huawei dependency should be explicitly addressed in the SOV scoring (SOV-1, SOV-5) and the financial chapter — if the thesis finds T-Cloud Public scores highly on sovereignty criteria, the Huawei technology dependency should be a noted qualification. |
