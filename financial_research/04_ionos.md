## 2. Business Model Context

> **Important for thesis interpretation:** IONOS is primarily a web hosting, domains, and digital services provider for small and medium-sized businesses (SMBs) — not a pure-play IaaS cloud provider. Its business model is closer to GoDaddy or Web.com than to AWS or OVHcloud.

In 2025, IONOS restructured its segment reporting to reflect this distinction:

| Segment (FY2025) | Revenue | % of Total | Description |
|-----------------|---------|------------|-------------|
| Web Presence & Productivity | ~€1.1B | 83% | Domains, website builder, email, managed hosting, Microsoft 365 resale |
| Cloud Solutions | ~€187M | 14% | Dedicated servers, VPS, S3-compatible storage, cloud hosting |
| *AdTech (Sedo) — discontinued* | *~€291M* | *n/a* | *Domain parking, auction platform — classified as discontinued operation, sale pending Q3 2026* |

> **Key finding:** IONOS's pure cloud revenue (Cloud Solutions segment, €187M) is smaller than OVHcloud's Public Cloud segment (€219.2M in FY2025). IONOS's headline revenue (€1,317M continuing) is larger than OVHcloud (€1,085M) but predominantly from non-cloud, SMB digital services.

> **Forward investment assessment:** IONOS's investment trajectory is constrained by its services-overlay model — low capex (€65M total, 5% of revenue) means it cannot independently match the infrastructure commitments of OVHcloud, StackIT, or Scaleway. Its IPCEI-CIS participation provides access to federated EU cloud infrastructure it could not build alone, which is strategically significant given its limited capex envelope. Not winning the EC sovereign cloud tender is a signal worth noting for the thesis: the Commission's procurement decision implicitly ranked OVHcloud, STACKIT, and Scaleway as more aligned with its sovereignty criteria for the institutional market.
>
> The Sedo AdTech divestiture (target Q3 2026 close) is the key near-term event — it will free management bandwidth and could redirect capital toward the Cloud Solutions segment, but the asset base and capex model will remain structurally light.

## 12. Summary Assessment for Thesis

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Revenue data availability** | Good — publicly listed, annual reports and investor presentations available. Segment restructuring (2024 → 2025) requires care in time-series comparisons. |
| **Scale (cloud-relevant revenue)** | IONOS Cloud Solutions (IaaS/cloud): **€187M (FY2025)** — this is the figure most comparable to OVHcloud's Public Cloud (€219M) and AWS. Total continuing-operations revenue (€1,317M) is dominated by SMB web services, not cloud infrastructure. |
| **Growth rate** | Like-for-like continuing operations: +5.5% (FY2025). Total group (inc. AdTech): CAGR 2020–2024 ~12.1%. Slowing as the business matures. |
| **Investment intensity** | Very low capex (5% of revenue) — IONOS is a cloud services overlay, not an infrastructure builder. This is structurally different from OVHcloud or StackIT. |
| **Financial stability** | Strong — conservative leverage (1.4×), €308M free cash flow, United Internet as a financially strong parent. The Sedo divestiture, if completed, would reduce revenue but improve margin quality. |
| **EU sovereignty position** | IONOS operates entirely within EU/EEA data centres and complies with GDPR. However, it does not hold SecNumCloud or equivalent national-level sovereign cloud certification. Its CLOUD Act exposure is lower than AWS/Azure (German parent, no US government contracts) but it is not a recognised sovereign cloud provider in the ENISA/EUCS framework. |
| **Thesis implication** | IONOS is primarily an SMB web services provider with a growing cloud segment — not a direct participant in the EU sovereign cloud market for regulated enterprise workloads. Its €187M cloud segment competes at the lower end of the cloud market (SME, developer, affordable VPS). The financial comparison to AWS should focus on the cloud segment only; the total revenue comparison is misleading without the context of what that revenue represents. |
